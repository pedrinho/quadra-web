How it works

Cells remember their piece

Every cell knows which of its edges are joined to the piece it arrived in. The bevels on the board are that record: an edge is drawn wherever a cell is not joined to its neighbour.

A clear cuts pieces apart

Clearing a line removes the row and breaks the joins across it. A piece that spanned the line is cut into fragments that no longer hold together.

What is left falls as one body

Anything unsupported drops as a single rigid group, not row by row. On the way down it can complete more lines, and those clears cascade.

Every score on the leaderboard is a recording. The server replays it through the same engine before it counts, so a score can only be played, not typed in. The engine is a bit-exact port of the 1998 C++, tested against the original game.
